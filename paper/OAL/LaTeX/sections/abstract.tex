On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student on its own sampled trajectories using dense token-level preferences from a stronger teacher. This provides fine-grained supervision on student-visited states, but it also assumes that every teacher logit difference is reliable along the whole sampled trajectory. In long-horizon reasoning, this assumption can fail: a local teacher preference may conflict with the final correctness of the response, and later prefixes may drift into regions where teacher guidance is less trustworthy. We propose \emph{Prefix-Outcome Validation} (POV), a validation layer for OPD that calibrates dense teacher supervision using two complementary signals. Outcome validation uses final correctness to determine whether the teacher--student log-probability gap of each sampled token is aligned with the trajectory result, preserving aligned tokens while assigning only beta-capped rank weights to anti-aligned tokens. Prefix validation monitors teacher excess surprisal along the generated prefix and monotonically decays token supervision after sustained prefix drift. POV preserves OPD's dense supervision while grounding it in both final outcome consistency and prefix-level teacher support, yielding a conservative alternative to outcome-only RL and unvalidated dense distillation. Experiments across multiple reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that POV provides more reliable credit assignment than OPD.
